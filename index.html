Your mole mapping visit, step by step

Consultation with Dr K

Your appointment begins with a personal consultation — Dr K conducts the entire mapping process himself. A chaperone is available, and a family member is welcome to attend.

Automated total body photography

An automatic lift positions the camera to your height and body shape, capturing four rapid photos of each side of your body.

Digital stitching & dermoscopy

Photos are stitched into a comprehensive whole-body view with detailed pictures of each mole. Any mole of concern is captured in extra detail with microimaging.

Same-visit review & report

Dr K reviews the images with you — confirming no moles of concern, or identifying any that need review, with a report for NHS or private referral.

Personalised follow-up plan

Based on your risk, we recommend when to return — 3–6 months or annually. Repeat visits highlight new or changed moles, essential for early detection.

Neurodiversity pathway

How an assessment works

Referral or self-referral

Contact us directly or be referred by your GP or school. We gather GP details, consent and any previous reports before booking.

Pre-assessment information

Developmental and medical history from parents or carers, school and teacher collateral, rating scales, and any previous CAMHS, paediatric, psychology or EHCP reports.

Specialist diagnostic assessment

A full clinical assessment with the Consultant Child & Adolescent Psychiatrist — developmental and psychiatric history, observer reports and mental state assessment.

Formulation and written report

Diagnostic conclusion against recognised criteria, functional impact across settings, coexisting conditions, strengths and support needs, plus school recommendations and a treatment plan.

Treatment and follow-up

Where medication is appropriate, initiation, titration and monitoring are provided in clinic. Shared-care prescribing by an NHS GP depends on the individual practice and local NHS arrangements, and cannot be guaranteed.

What makes us different

Skin health — the gap in most corporate medicals

Skin cancer is one of the most common cancers in the UK, and cases continue to rise — yet most workplace health programmes never look at the skin at all. That gap leaves employees at risk, and early detection is the single biggest factor in outcomes.

  • Most corporate medicals stop at heart, bloods and lifestyle — skin is rarely checked
  • Employees who work outdoors or travel frequently face higher UV exposure
  • Caught early, skin cancer is highly treatable — late detection changes everything
  • Our packages can include digital full-body mole mapping — expert dermoscopy by an international specialist, with a baseline record for future comparison
  • Personalised recall reminders so no employee falls through the gap
